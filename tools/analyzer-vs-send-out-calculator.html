Buying an amino acid analyzer? What to consider first

What to weigh before you commit, and a free calculator to compare in-house cost per sample with sending samples to a lab.

Updated November 26, 2026Free tool
Short answer

Before you buy, decide what you must measure, how many samples you will run each month, and who will run and review them. Then compare the full cost per sample, not just the instrument quote, with the price of sending samples out. Below volumes where the instrument stays busy, sending samples out is usually the lower-risk choice for data quality.

What to consider before you buy

  • What you must measure. Free amino acids and total amino acids are different analyses. Check which analytes you need: cysteine/cystine and tryptophan need special handling, and after acid hydrolysis asparagine and glutamine are reported with aspartate and glutamate.
  • Chemistry and detection. Pre-column derivatization with UV or fluorescence detection runs on an HPLC or UHPLC. Dedicated post-column ninhydrin analyzers are a separate, purpose-built approach. Each has its own consumables, upkeep and skill needs.
  • Hydrolysis capacity. Total amino acids need acid hydrolysis first (6 N HCl, 110 °C, 24 h). Make sure you have the hydrolysis equipment and space for the sample numbers you expect.
  • Samples per month and break-even. A fixed instrument and service cost is spread over your volume, so the cost per sample falls as volume rises. The calculator below finds your break-even point.
  • Staff skill and hands-on time. Count sample prep, instrument runs and data review, and who covers when your trained person is away.
  • Calibration, QC and record-keeping every run. Standards, control samples and run records are needed with every run, and they take time and consumables.
  • Validation. If your results support regulated decisions, plan for method validation before the first reportable result.
  • Downtime and backup capacity. When the instrument is down or backlogged, samples still arrive. Decide where they go.
  • Full cost per sample. Add the instrument, the service contract, consumables and staff time. That is what the calculator does with your own quotes.

How the math works

  1. Fixed cost per month. Spread the instrument price over its useful life, add the yearly service contract, and divide by 12.
  2. Cost per sample in-house. Divide the monthly fixed cost by your samples per month, then add consumables and staff time (hours per sample × hourly cost).
  3. Compare with sending out. OpenChemWorks AA-1 list pricing is $150 per sample, $125 per sample at 15 or more samples a month, and $99 per sample at 30 or more a month.
  4. Break-even. The fixed cost is the same however many samples you run, so the in-house cost per sample falls as volume rises. Break-even is the lowest monthly volume at which in-house cost per sample is at or below the send-out price for that volume.

What the numbers leave out

The tool compares running costs only. In practice an in-house instrument also needs lab space, method set-up and validation time, instrument downtime, and a person who knows the chemistry. Those costs are real and differ from lab to lab, so add them to staff hours or service cost if they matter to you. On the send-out side, AA-1 list prices cover a standard liquid sample. Solids and powders that need extraction add $30 per sample (PREP-1), and the minimum order is $180.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What should I consider before buying an amino acid analyzer?

Decide what you must measure (free or total amino acids, and which analytes), how many samples you will run each month, and who will prepare, run and review them. Also plan for hydrolysis capacity, calibration and QC every run, validation if results support regulated decisions, backup for downtime, and the full cost per sample.

How much does an amino acid analyzer cost?

It depends on the vendor and the configuration, and we do not quote vendor prices. The instrument price is only part of the cost: service, consumables and staff time add to it. Get quotes and enter them in the calculator on this page.

When is sending samples out cheaper than buying?

When your monthly volume is below the break-even point, because the instrument and service cost are fixed and are spread over few samples. The calculator on this page finds that volume from your own quotes. Our prices are $150 per sample, $125 at 15 or more a month and $99 at 30 or more a month.

Can I send samples while my instrument is down or backlogged?

Yes. Overnight turnaround is available, with expedited service on request. The $250 PILOT lets new customers compare our results with their own on 5 samples first.
