How the conversion works, in plain words

A micromolar value counts molecules. A mg/L value weighs them. The molar mass connects the two: one micromole of an amino acid weighs its molar mass in micrograms. So converting from µM to mg/L means multiplying by the molar mass and dividing by 1,000.

  1. Apply the dilution. If you diluted the sample before measuring, multiply the measured value by the dilution factor to get back to the sample as received.
  2. µM to mg/L. mg/L = µM × molar mass ÷ 1,000. The reverse is µM = mg/L × 1,000 ÷ molar mass.
  3. mg/L to mg/100 mL. Divide by 10, because 100 mL is one tenth of a litre.
  4. mg/L to mg/g of solid. mg/g = mg/L × extract volume in litres ÷ sample mass in g. This needs the mass you extracted and the volume you extracted it into.

Worked example

A spent-medium sample was diluted 1 part in 10 and read 50 µM glutamine on the diluted sample. Glutamine has a molar mass of 146.14 g/mol.

  • As received: 50 µM × 10 = 500 µM.
  • In mass units: 500 × 146.14 ÷ 1,000 = 73.07 mg/L.
  • Per 100 mL: 73.07 ÷ 10 = 7.307 mg/100 mL.
  • If the same number had come from 1.0 g of powder extracted into 50 mL: 73.07 mg/L × 0.050 L ÷ 1.0 g = about 3.65 mg/g.

Which basis the tool uses

The table uses free amino acid molar masses, the form measured in AA-1 free amino acid analysis. Inside a protein, each amino acid loses a water molecule (about 18.02 g/mol) when it joins the chain, so residue masses are lower. If you are converting total amino acid results after acid hydrolysis, see total amino acid analysis, which reports g/100 g and mol%. Hydrolysis changes some amino acids: tryptophan is destroyed, cysteine and cystine are not reported in AA-T, and asparagine and glutamine are reported as aspartate and glutamate.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula to convert µM to mg/L for an amino acid?

mg/L = µM × molar mass ÷ 1,000. For glutamine (146.14 g/mol), 500 µM is 73.07 mg/L.

Does the converter use free amino acid or residue molar masses?

It uses free amino acid molar masses, which is what free amino acid analysis measures. Residue masses inside a protein are about 18.02 g/mol lower because water is lost when the chain forms.

How do I correct for a dilution?

Multiply the value you measured by the dilution factor. A 1 in 10 dilution has a factor of 10. Reports from OpenChemWorks are already corrected back for in-house dilution.

What do I need to get mg/g?

The mass of solid you extracted and the volume you extracted it into. mg/g = mg/L × extract volume in litres ÷ sample mass in grams.

Which amino acids are in the AA-1 panel?

AA-1 reports 19 amino acids plus ammonia in µM and mg/L: alanine, arginine, asparagine, aspartate, glutamate, glutamine, glycine, histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, proline, serine, threonine, tryptophan, tyrosine and valine.
