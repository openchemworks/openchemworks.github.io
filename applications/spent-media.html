What spent media tells you

A spent sample is typically a clarified supernatant drawn at a stated day and condition. A day 7 sample and a day 12 sample from the same run are different statements, so sampling discipline defines its value. Three readings do most of the work.

  • Depletion. Amino acids falling toward exhaustion are candidate limits on the culture and candidates for feed attention.
  • Persistence. Amino acids that stay high throughout were supplied beyond what the culture used, a cost with no benefit.
  • The glutamine and ammonia pair. Cells consume glutamine, and it also decomposes chemically to ammonia. Ammonia accumulation is associated with inhibited CHO growth (Schneider, Marison and von Stockar, Journal of Biotechnology, 1996). We report the pair as measured and do not attribute a batch result to ammonia without your culture data. See measuring ammonia in cell culture and glutamine in spent media.

Caveats for culture media

Alanyl-glutamine is not free glutamine

Many media supply glutamine as the dipeptide alanyl-glutamine (GlutaMAX). In a 21-day CAR-T expansion it was entirely cleaved over the run (Jensen et al., Scientific Reports, 2025). AA-1 reports free glutamine and free alanine. The intact dipeptide is not counted as glutamine, so low free glutamine early in a dipeptide-fed culture does not by itself mean the glutamine source is used up.

Freeze promptly and keep tubes capped

Ammonia is volatile. In a CAR-T sampling study, samples kept in a refrigerator had significantly lower ammonia than samples frozen immediately, and the authors suggest ammonia evaporated during storage (Chan et al., Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2025). Freeze samples promptly after collection, keep them capped, and store every sample in a time course the same way.

Glutamine breaks down without any cells

Glutamine in medium turns into ammonia on its own. In one study, pre-warmed or room-temperature medium produced over 20-fold more ammonium than fresh medium, while media without L-glutamine or with GlutaMAX stayed close to the detection limit (Kegel et al., Virology, 2025). Send a fresh sample handled the same way as your culture samples, so the chemical share of the ammonia can be separated from what the cells made.

Serum, platelet lysate and albumin

Serum, platelet lysate, albumin and cytokine carriers add protein, and protein carries amine groups that react with AQC. We remove protein before derivatization, and a PREP-2 spike-recovery check ($200 per assay, once per formulation, waived at 20+ samples) confirms recovery the first time we see a new medium. Cysteine and cystine are not in AA-1. AA-3 covers them, priced after a free call. Asparagine can convert to aspartate on storage, and the profile will show it.

FAQ

Why not just analyze the fresh medium?
Fresh analysis verifies what you made. It cannot show what the cells consumed or what built up. Spent analysis is the consumption record, and the difference between the two is the insight.

How many time points should I send?
Enough to show the shape of depletion for your question. A single endpoint shows leftovers, and a series shows curves. RUN-1 covers one run with up to 24 time points for $1,990.

Does high ammonia explain my viability dip?
Not from our report alone. Ammonia is associated with growth inhibition in the literature, but attributing a specific event needs your culture data next to the profile.

Does AA-1 count alanyl-glutamine as glutamine?
No. AA-1 reports free glutamine and free alanine. The intact dipeptide is not counted, so read early glutamine values with that in mind.

How should I store spent media samples?
Freeze them promptly after collection, keep the tubes capped, and store every sample in a time course the same way. Ammonia is volatile.

Are you accredited (ISO 17025, GLP or GMP)?
Not yet. Our testing supports research, product development and process monitoring. Every report includes the chromatogram, calibration and QC results, so you can see exactly how your numbers were made.

Is an at-line analyzer cheaper than sending spent media out?
Only at steady, high volume. Below that, sending samples out per run is often cheaper, and it gives you the full amino acid profile with ammonia and the chromatogram behind every number. The analyzer vs send-out calculator compares the two at your volume.
