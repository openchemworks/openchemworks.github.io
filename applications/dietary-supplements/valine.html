BCAA testing for dietary supplements

Published November 11, 2026Updated November 19, 20264 min readOpenChemWorks Laboratory
Short answer

For a BCAA supplement, run AA-1 once. It reports leucine, isoleucine and valine as separate results in the same run, so you can check each against the label per serving and check the ratio. AA-1 is $150 per sample, plus $30 for capsules, tablets and powders that need weighing and extraction. Use AA-T only for a protein-based product, and read total valine and isoleucine as minimums.

Which test answers a BCAA question

Added BCAAs in a capsule, tablet, powder or drink are free amino acids, so the free panel (AA-1) answers the label question directly. Total (AA-T) answers a protein composition question and includes free plus protein-bound amino acids. Comparing a free result with a total-protein claim is a common source of false alarms, so state which claim you are checking.

The tests named here are part of our amino acid analysis service.

Per-serving math

Results come as mg/g or g/100 g. Multiply by the serving size to compare with the label. Worked arithmetic only: a powder that measures 400 mg/g leucine with a 5 g serving gives 400 x 5 = 2,000 mg leucine per serving. Say whether the label states each BCAA as the free amino acid or as a salt or blend.

Check the ratio

Many labels state a ratio such as 2:1:1 (leucine : isoleucine : valine, by mass). Divide each measured amount per serving by the isoleucine or valine amount. Worked arithmetic only: 2.0 g leucine, 1.0 g isoleucine and 1.0 g valine per serving is 2:1:1, while 2.0 g, 0.8 g and 0.7 g is about 2:0.8:0.7. A ratio off the label is a question to take up with the maker. Leucine, isoleucine and valine are never reported as a combined BCAA total.

What to send

Send the finished product as sold: capsules and tablets in their container, powder in a clean sealed container, liquids sealed. Label each with product name and lot, mix powders thoroughly first, and include the exact per-serving claim for each BCAA. Capsule shells, binders, coatings, flavours and sweeteners are part of the sample as sold.

Hydrolysis and BCAAs

What hydrolysis does to BCAAs. Valine and isoleucine bonds (Val-Val, Val-Ile, Ile-Ile) hydrolyse slowly, so a 24 h hydrolysis can under-recover them in a protein sample. Leucine is released in the standard 24 h hydrolysis. Free AA-1 results are not hydrolysed and are not affected. Asparagine and glutamine are reported with aspartic and glutamic acid, and tryptophan is destroyed, but neither touches the BCAAs.

What the report shows

Per-analyte results in the units you need, the chromatogram, and the calibration and QC for the run. Leucine, isoleucine and valine are resolved as separate peaks in the same run. Keep panel, form and basis the same across samples in a comparison set. Performance figures come from the published method; every report includes our own calibration and QC for the run. Reports also show AABA, the internal standard we add to every sample. It is not a result for your sample.

Frequently asked questions

Do I run AA-1 or AA-T for a BCAA supplement?
AA-1 for added free BCAAs in a capsule, tablet, powder or drink. AA-T only for a protein-based product, where the question is what the protein contains.

Is the BCAA result a combined total?
No. Leucine, isoleucine and valine are reported as separate results in the same run. Add them yourself if the label states a total.

Why can total valine and isoleucine read low?
Valine and isoleucine bonds release slowly in 24 h acid hydrolysis, so a total result can under-recover them in a protein sample. Free AA-1 results are not affected.

Does a result show that a BCAA product works?
No. A result shows how much of each amino acid was in the sample. Composition is not efficacy.

Can I test leucine or isoleucine alone?
They come in the same AA-1 run as valine, so you get all three for the price of one sample.
