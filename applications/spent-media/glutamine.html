Applications

Glutamine in spent media

Published November 4, 2026Updated November 19, 20264 min readOpenChemWorks Laboratory
Short answer

Free glutamine in spent media is measured in AA-1 without hydrolysis. Read it only by comparison within your own process: against your starting formulation, an earlier sample from the same run, or a parallel condition. Total analysis does not report glutamine separately, because hydrolysis converts it to glutamic acid.

What glutamine means in spent media

Spent media has supported a culture, so its composition records what the culture took, what it left, and what built up. Glutamine is central to that record for two reasons a single result cannot separate.

Cells use glutamine, the amide form of glutamic acid, as a nitrogen carrier, so free glutamine remaining at sampling describes in part what a culture has used and left. Glutamine also changes without any cell action: in aqueous solution and stored media it changes over time, and it decomposes chemically to pyroglutamate and ammonia. Ammonia in a spent sample can therefore come from both cell metabolism and glutamine decomposition. Read the pair together. Glutamine is not glutamate, not glutathione, and not ammonia, which AA-1 reports separately.

No glutamine or ammonia threshold is set here, for culture health or any go or no-go decision. A threshold fitting one process can mislead in another, so judgement stays with your own process history and culture data.

Sample preparation

Spent media is a complex matrix. A clarified supernatant drawn at a stated day and condition can still carry debris and protein unless clarified, so preparation is part of the measurement and is recorded. Send it as a clarified liquid where you can, and state the culture day, the run it came from, and whether a matched fresh sample is included.

Because glutamine changes in solution, the interval and conditions between sampling and measurement affect the result, which describes the sample as received. Tell the lab when the sample was taken and how it was stored, and ask what handling it recommends before shipping. Email before sending frozen samples so arrival handling is agreed. Pyroglutamate is not a panel analyte; the panel tracks glutamine loss and ammonia rise. Where a decision depends closely on the value, ask about matrix-effect qualification, checking distortion with a known spike in your sample, on inquiry.

Reporting bases and what the report shows

Glutamine is reported free, measured directly without hydrolysis: consumption acts on the free pool.

The report lists each amino acid individually, plus ammonia, on a stated reporting basis, with the chromatogram behind each number available. For a time course, results are presented sample by sample in the order and labelling you supplied. Where the matrix may affect recovery, spike recovery and matrix-effect qualification belong in the work, and the report states what was and was not qualified.

Do not ask a total report to split native glutamine back out.

What hydrolysis does to glutamine. Under 6 N HCl at 110 C the amide group is lost and glutamine becomes glutamic acid, so a total report cannot show native glutamine. Use AA-1. Pyroglutamate has no free amine to tag, so the panel follows glutamine loss and ammonia rise rather than measuring pyroglutamate itself.

Preparing the supernatant

Clarify the sample first: spin out cells and debris, or filter, then freeze the supernatant. Tell us what you did. Note the culture day and the run for every tube, and include a matched fresh-medium sample if you can.

Frequently asked questions

Why read glutamine with ammonia?
Ammonia here comes from both cell metabolism and glutamine decomposition, so the pair is more informative than either line alone. No threshold is applied to either.

Can a total test give me glutamine?
No, not separately. Hydrolysis converts it to glutamic acid, folding it into the glutamate line. Use free AA-1.

Is a low result a problem?
That depends on your process and comparators. No universal cut-off is given, and a result alone does not decide culture health or a batch.

How many time points should I send?
Enough to show the shape of change across the run.

Is cysteine included?
No. Cysteine is excluded from AA-1 and needs a separate AA-3 workup.
