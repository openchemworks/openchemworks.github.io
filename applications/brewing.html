Amino acid analysis for brewing

Brewers send wort and beer for amino acid analysis when a fermentation behaved differently than the recipe predicted — a sluggish start, an attenuation shift after a malt or adjunct change, or a flavour difference between two batches that looked identical on paper. Yeast does not consume "nitrogen" in the abstract. It takes up individual amino acids, and the mix in your wort shapes how fermentation proceeds. This page is for head brewers, QA staff, and brewery founders who want that mix measured rather than inferred.

What this sample type is

Wort is a sugar-rich, protein-bearing liquid made from malt and adjuncts. Its free amino acid content comes from malt modification and mashing, and it changes again through the boil, fermentation, and conditioning. Beer is the same matrix after yeast has taken what it used and left the rest, plus whatever yeast itself released.

That history is the point of the sample. A wort profile is a starting condition. A beer profile is an outcome. Comparing the two, from the same brew, shows which amino acids yeast actually consumed and which it left — a different and more useful picture than either sample alone. Alcohol, carbonation, sugars, and hop compounds make beer a real analytical matrix, not flavoured water, so preparation and matrix checks matter and are stated in the report.

What we measure in it

Our free panel, AA-1, measures 19 amino acids plus ammonia by AQC (AccQ-Tag) pre-column derivatization with HPLC-UV. In wort and beer, the free panel is normally the relevant one: it reports the amino acids present in free form, which is the form yeast encounters.

Ammonia is reported alongside the amino acids because it is part of the nitrogen picture yeast works with — the panel treats it as a measured analyte, not an afterthought. Proline deserves a plain note: brewers often ask about it specifically because its behaviour in fermentation differs from most other amino acids. If proline, or any single analyte, is the reason for your order, tell us before ordering so we can confirm what the panel reports for this matrix.

A total amino acid run (AA-T) is rarely the brewing question. It becomes relevant for raw materials — for example, the protein composition of a malt or adjunct ingredient — rather than for wort or finished beer.

Cysteine is excluded from AA-1 and requires a separate AA-3 workup.

Sample and submission

Send wort or beer as a liquid, in the condition that represents the stage you are asking about — a fermentation sample pulled mid-ferment answers a different question than a packaged beer, and the report will name the stage you state. Follow the amount, container, and packaging guidance on the live sample-submission page; no different amount is stated here. Email info@openchemworks.com before sending carbonated or actively fermenting samples so handling on arrival is agreed first.

What the report shows

The report lists each measured amino acid individually, plus ammonia, on a stated reporting basis, with the chromatogram behind each number available. For a wort-and-beer pair submitted together, results are presented so the before-and-after comparison is readable sample by sample. Where a matrix may affect recovery, spike recovery and matrix-effect qualification belong in the work for that sample, and the report states what was and was not qualified. The report reports concentrations. It does not diagnose why a fermentation behaved as it did — that interpretation stays with the brewer, using the numbers as evidence.

FAQ

Is this the same as a FAN measurement? No. A single free-amino-nitrogen figure sums a response; AA-1 separates and reports individual amino acids plus ammonia, so you can see which ones moved.

Should I send wort, beer, or both? Both, if the question is what yeast consumed. Wort alone describes the starting condition; beer alone describes what was left.

Do you cover wine on this page? No — wine sits on the existing wine and brewing / wine-and-fermentation pages linked below. This hub is brewing only.

Are you an accredited brewing lab? No. We are not GLP, GMP, or ISO 17025 accredited, and we do not imply otherwise.
