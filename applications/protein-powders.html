Amino Acid Analysis for Protein Powders, Bars and Shakes

Protein brands and ingredient buyers send us powders, bars and shakes to check that the protein is real and the profile matches the label. AA-T gives the profile, and AA-1 shows added free amino acids. Tryptophan and cysteine are not reported by AA-T.

Is the protein real?

Two tests answer this, and they answer different halves.

  • AA-T (total, $230) gives g/100 g for 16 amino acids. Add them up and compare the sum with the label protein, then check that the pattern looks like the stated source. The sum comes in a little under the true protein, because tryptophan and cysteine are not reported. A sum far below the label points to low protein or non-protein bulk.
  • AA-1 (free, $150) shows amino acids added as free powders, often glycine, leucine or taurine, which raise the total without adding protein. Intact protein releases almost no free amino acid, so a large free peak stands out. Creatine is not an amino acid and is measured separately with CR-1 ($120).

Collagen has essentially no tryptophan, so the missing tryptophan matters little there. For whey or pea it matters more. Our guides on declared versus delivered protein and collagen and complete protein go further.

What AA-T reports and what it does not, for protein powders

  • AA-T reports 16 amino acids after 6 N HCl hydrolysis at 110 °C for 24 hours, in g/100 g and mol%.
  • Tryptophan is destroyed by the acid and is not reported.
  • Cysteine and cystine are not reported in AA-T. Cysteine needs the AA-3 workup.
  • Asparagine and glutamine convert to aspartate and glutamate, so they are reported with those two.
  • Methionine can partly oxidize, so it can read low.
  • Serine and threonine are partly lost during hydrolysis.
  • Valine and isoleucine bonds hydrolyze slowly, so 24 hours can under-recover them.

Finished products: bars and shakes

A bar or shake is a finished food. Protein is mixed with fats, sugars, fibers and flavors, and processing changes it. Test the product as sold.

  • Bars are dense solids. Homogenize several bars together before taking the portion, and send at least 100 mg per sample. Bars add PREP-1 at $30.
  • Ready-to-drink shakes are emulsified liquids. Send 200 µL per sample, with 400 µL preferred. Liquids are $150 for AA-1.
  • Dry shake mixes price as powders.

Lysine reacts with sugars during baking and storage (the Maillard reaction). Acid hydrolysis converts part of those early products back to lysine, so total lysine can read higher than the lysine your body can use. Compare lots on the same test and basis, and treat a lysine trend as a signal rather than an availability value.

How much to send

Send at least 100 mg of well-mixed powder or homogenized bar per sample. Scooping from one corner of a tub may not represent the lot, so mix first. Liquids need 200 µL, with 400 µL preferred. Label each tube with the sample ID exactly as it appears on your sample sheet, and note the source (whey, plant blend, collagen) and the claim you are checking. Book online first. FAQ

Free or total for a protein powder?
Total (AA-T) for the protein profile and for comparing amino acids with the label protein. Free (AA-1) to find added free amino acids or to check a free amino acid claim.

How do I tell if a powder has added free amino acids?
Run AA-1. Free glycine, leucine, taurine or similar at high levels means they were added, because intact protein releases almost none. A total amino acid sum above what the protein source could give points the same way.

Can you tell me if my powder is complete?
We report the measured profile. AA-T does not report tryptophan or cysteine, so a full essential amino acid score needs those from other work. Whether a profile meets a definition you use is your call.

Should I test the finished bar or shake, or the powder?
Test the product you sell. Ingredient results answer an ingredient question, and a baked bar can differ from the powder it started as.

Does flavor or coating matter?
It is part of the sample. Send the exact flavor and format you need a decision on.

Are you accredited (ISO 17025, GLP or GMP)?
Not yet. Our testing supports research, product development and process monitoring. Every report includes the chromatogram, calibration and QC results, so you can see exactly how your numbers were made.
