Amino acid analysis for protein powders

If you sell, formulate, or buy protein powder, the amino acid profile is the detail behind the headline protein number. Brands, formulators, and ingredient buyers send us whey, plant, and blended powders when they need to verify a profile, compare suppliers, or check that a finished powder still matches its specification. The result feeds a clear decision: release the lot, reformulate, or renegotiate with evidence.

What this sample type is

Protein powders look simple and are not. A whey isolate, a pea-rice blend, and a collagen powder are all dry powders, but their amino acid patterns differ by source, and two powders with the same total protein can have quite different individual patterns. That is why the profile matters more than the total alone.

These samples need powder preparation and thorough mixing: scooping from one corner of a tub may not represent the lot. Reference data for protein ingredients is available through USDA FoodData Central. It describes generic ingredients, not your finished, flavored product.

What we measure in it

We measure amino acids using AQC (AccQ-Tag) pre-column derivatization with HPLC and fluorescence detection. For protein powders, you need to decide which question you are asking, because free and total analysis answer different ones.

Free amino acid analysis (AA-1) measures the amino acids already free in the powder. That is the right question if the powder contains added free amino acids, or if you want to check a free-amino claim. Total amino acid analysis (AA-T) hydrolyzes the protein first, so it measures free plus protein-bound amino acids together. That is usually the right question for verifying the overall profile of the protein itself, including branched-chain and other essential amino acids as part of the full AA-1 analyte set after hydrolysis.

Many powder questions need total, not free. If you are unsure, tell us the label claim or specification you are checking before you order, and we will point you to the panel that actually answers it. Ordering the wrong form wastes the sample.

Sample and submission

Send the powder in its dry, as-sold form, well mixed, in a clean, sealed container labeled with the product name and lot. Follow the amount guidance on our sample-submission page, if you are unsure how much to send, email info@openchemworks.com first rather than guessing.

Mail-in submission is available. Contact us before shipping so your sample is expected. Tell us the source (for example, whey, plant blend, or collagen), the claim or specification you are checking, and whether you need free, total, or advice on which, that context determines how the sample is set up.

What the report shows

The report shows per-analyte results, the reporting basis used, and the chromatogram behind each number. For total analysis, results reflect hydrolyzed, protein-bound plus free amino acids together, not a free-amino result, and not comparable to one as if they were the same.

For lot or supplier comparisons, keep the panel, form, and basis the same across samples, and say at submission that samples are a comparison set. If a specific analyte drives your decision, raise it at submission so its treatment under hydrolysis is explicit in the report notes.

FAQ

For a protein powder label check, do I want free or total?
Usually total (AA-T), because most label profile claims describe the protein itself. Free (AA-1) fits added free amino acids or free-amino claims. Tell us the claim and we will confirm.

Can you tell me if my powder is "complete"?
We report the measured profile. Whether a profile meets a definition you use is your call to make from those numbers, we do not certify completeness.

Does flavor or sweetener in the powder matter?
It is part of the matrix, so list the full product as sold when you submit. Do not send a base powder if the decision is about the finished, flavored product.

Is cysteine included?
No. Cysteine is excluded from AA-1 and requires a separate AA-3 workup.

Are you accredited (ISO 17025, GLP or GMP)?
Not yet. Our testing supports research, product development and process monitoring. Every report includes the chromatogram, calibration and QC results, so you can see exactly how your numbers were made.
