Short answer

A spent-media panel measures 19 free amino acids plus ammonia in one run, showing which amino acids your culture consumed and how much ammonia built up, per batch and time point. Every report includes the chromatogram. Prices are $150 per sample, $125 at 25+ a month and $99 on standing contract.

Feed decisions made on guesswork.

You adjust the feed because the last batch worked — or because a paper suggested an additive, or because the vendor’s application note said so. The culture tells you the outcome weeks later. It never tells you what it needed.

The instrument vendors will happily sell you the tools to measure amino acids yourself. A column here, a kit there, a method to develop on your own time. What they don’t sell is the answer.

So the most expensive variable in your process — the feed — gets optimized by trial and error.

Latest research

New papers on this topic, newest first. We check PubMed every month; last checked October 2026.

  • Apr 2026 Proteomic analysis of ammonia-induced stress in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell cultures J Proteomics

    Adding 10 or 30 mM ammonia suppressed CHO growth and titer. ER proteomics showed effects on the TCA cycle and intracellular trafficking proteins.

  • Mar 2026 Amino acid metabolism, demand and supply in Chinese Hamster ovary cell culture - A comprehensive literature review Biotechnol Adv

    Reviews CHO amino acid demand and supply across fed-batch culture, including toxicity, solubility, stability, transporters and strategies to balance media formulation.

  • Oct 2025 Optimization of amino acid composition in CHO cell perfusion medium using definitive screening design and 1H NMR-based consumption profiling Biotechnol Prog

    NMR uptake profiling picked nine key amino acids for a CHO perfusion medium. Optimizing them kept viability high and lowered lactate and ammonium.
