Short answer

Two pre-analytical choices routinely create false group differences in plasma amino acid studies before samples ever reach the HPLC column: leaving whole blood at room temperature past 2 hours drops glutamine while artificially inflating glutamate, and mixing 10 kDa centrifugal ultrafiltration with direct sulfosalicylic acid (SSA) precipitation measures two different tryptophan pools because 70% to 90% of plasma tryptophan binds albumin.

This guide reviews published analytical literature to help research teams standardize cohort collection. No clinical outcomes or patient studies are implied.

1. Pre-centrifugation whole-blood delays drive Gln-to-Glu drift within 2 hours

In multi-site preclinical and nutrition studies, blood tubes often wait on a bench or travel between animal rooms and the centrifuge. Kamlage et al. (2014, Clinical Chemistry) showed that ongoing metabolism in unseparated blood cells and platelet activation alter plasma amino acid signatures within 2 hours at room temperature. Glutamine is particularly vulnerable to ex vivo conversion while glutamate increases concurrently, skewing both absolute glutamate levels and cohort Glu/Gln ratios.

Once blood is spun promptly in a refrigerated centrifuge and cleanly separated from cells, isolated plasma holds short-term stability much better, though prompt chilling on wet ice and storage at -80 C remain standard best practice. When comparing cohorts collected across clinics or bleeds, you can screen existing spreadsheets for elevated Glu/Gln artifact patterns in our free browser Plasma Amino Acid Ratio & Pre-Analytical QC Checker.

2. Deproteinization bias: 10 kDa ultrafiltration vs. SSA vs. organic precipitation

Removing plasma albumin and immunoglobulins protects reversed-phase C18 HPLC columns from fouling, yet different cleanup chemistries recover different fractions of bound amino acids, especially tryptophan (Trp), where 70% to 90% of circulating molecules reversibly bind serum albumin.

MethodMechanismTryptophan Pool & Recovery RiskColumn / Analytical Trade-off
5-Sulfosalicylic acid (SSA, 3% to 15% w/v)Chemical protein denaturation and acid precipitationDirect SSA can co-precipitate albumin-bound Trp unless pre-dissociated (e.g., SDS before SSA, Mondino et al. 1975 PMID 1150764)Strong acid/salt supernatant must be buffered cleanly for AQC/OPA derivatization
10 kDa Centrifugal UltrafiltrationPhysical MWCO size-exclusion membraneCaptures free (unbound) Trp only; hydrophobic indole side-chains risk membrane adsorptionResidual low-level protein leakage and polymer leachables can slowly foul columns or add baseline peaks
Cold Organic Solvent (ACN / MeOH)Solvent-induced protein crash + dissociationLiberates protein-bound Trp cleanly when calibrated with matched matrix standardsVolume dilution reduces raw signal slightly, demanding sensitive detection on micro-aliquots

Combining data generated after 10 kDa ultrafiltration with data generated after chemical deproteinization manufactures apparent 2-fold to 5-fold shifts in plasma tryptophan that reflect sample prep rather than biology. Even when comparing published lab reference ranges, interval definitions shift interpretations across analytes (Mayo vs. ARUP reference interval agreement map).
