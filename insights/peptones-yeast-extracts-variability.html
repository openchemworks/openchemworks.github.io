Summary: "peptone" is not an ingredient, it is a family

  • Across five manufacturer-published typical amino acid analyses, total reported amino acids range from 390 to 846 mg per gram of product, a 2.2-fold spread, before any lot-to-lot variation is even considered: soy peptone (ultrafiltered) 389.8 mg/g, soy peptone (standard) 484.8 mg/g, bacteriological yeast extract 524 mg/g, casein tryptone 781 mg/g, bacteriological (gelatin-rich) peptone 846.3 mg/g. All five are sold to do the same job in a medium.
  • The per-amino-acid spread is wider than the total spread. Glycine runs from 11 mg/g (yeast extract) to 207.1 mg/g (gelatin-rich bacteriological peptone), an 18.8-fold range. Proline runs 9 to 117.1 mg/g (13.0×). Lysine runs 18 to 60 mg/g (3.3×), methionine 6.5 to 17 mg/g (2.6×), leucine 29.4 to 70 mg/g (2.4×).
  • Most of what a peptone supplies is not free amino acid, and the free fraction varies by product class. From the three sheets that publish both total and free values: yeast extract's free amino acids are 51.1 mg/g, only 9.8% of its total; the two soy peptones are 183.1 mg/g (37.8%) and 145.0 mg/g (37.2%) free. A cell or microbe that takes up free amino acids sees a different medium than the total numbers suggest.
  • Switching between two soy peptones from the same manufacturer changes a defined medium's free amino acid supply by double-digit percentages. At 10 g/L, the standard soy peptone supplies 1,831 mg/L of free amino acids and the ultrafiltered version 1,450 mg/L, a difference of 381 mg/L (21%), including free leucine 290 vs 200 mg/L and free lysine 183 vs 126 mg/L. Same supplier, same nominal ingredient name, different process (ultrafiltration), different medium.

So what: when a fermentation or culture "behaves differently this lot," the first suspect is not the organism. It is the least characterised input in the recipe, and the recipe usually names it with one word.

Why it matters: media teams spec peptones by total nitrogen and a growth test. Neither number sees the amino acid supply, free or total, that the process actually consumes. Profiling an incoming lot, total and free, is a $150 measurement against a failed batch.

4. Worked example: the same-name switch, and a classic medium

Switch inside one supplier's soy family. Define a medium at 10 g/L soy peptone and change nothing but the grade, standard (P2) to ultrafiltered (P3):

Free amino acid supply at 10 g/L P2 standard P3 ultrafiltered Difference
All reported free amino acids 183.1 × 10 = 1,831 mg/L 145.0 × 10 = 1,450 mg/L −381 mg/L (−21%)
Free leucine 29.0 × 10 = 290 mg/L 20.0 × 10 = 200 mg/L −90 mg/L
Free lysine 18.3 × 10 = 183 mg/L 12.6 × 10 = 126 mg/L −57 mg/L
Free glutamic acid 19.1 × 10 = 191 mg/L 16.5 × 10 = 165 mg/L −26 mg/L
Free methionine 5.1 × 10 = 51 mg/L 3.9 × 10 = 39 mg/L −12 mg/L

Nothing in the recipe changed. The purchase order still says "soy peptone." The medium's immediately available amino acid supply fell by a fifth.

A classic recipe read through the sheets. A standard LB-style formulation (10 g/L tryptone + 5 g/L yeast extract) carries, from P4 and P1 typicals, a total leucine supply of 70 × 10 + 38 × 5 = 890 mg/L, of which the yeast extract's free leucine is only 4.6 × 5 = 23 mg/L; the tryptone sheet publishes no free fraction at all, so the free leucine of the complete medium cannot be computed from published data. That un-computability is the article's point in miniature: the most-used medium in biology has no published free amino acid budget.

5. Perspectives

  • Process: treat peptone grade changes, including "same product, new process step" changes like ultrafiltration, as medium changes. Re-validate; do not assume.
  • QC: an incoming-lot spec that can actually catch this is a total + free amino acid profile with acceptance bands on 3–4 process-critical amino acids (methionine and leucine are the usual suspects), not total nitrogen alone.
  • Procurement: a cheaper peptone is the same product only if the profile is. Compare cost per gram of the amino acids your process is limited by, not cost per kilogram of powder.
  • Analytical: total and free profiles answer different questions. Total predicts the nutrient ceiling; free predicts what is available before proteolysis. Hydrolysate products blur the two, which is why both are measured.

Methods & Sources

  • Manufacturer sheets (accessed November 5, 2026): Solabia Bacteriological Yeast Extract A120400 technical data sheet (v.02/2023): total and free amino acid distribution in mg/g; stated on the sheet to be typical analyses, not specifications. Nu-Tek Biosciences Soy Peptone HSP-N (1175_HSP-N_ProductSpec, 2025) and Soy Peptone HSP-A10KF (1000 product spec): typical amino acid profile, total and free, mg/g. Ausamics Peptone from Casein (Tryptone) AS-1006 technical data: amino acid composition mg/g. ITW Reagents Peptone, Bacteriological (403695) product info: amino acids in g/100 g, converted ×10 to mg/g.
  • Formulas: totals = sum of reported amino acids per sheet; free fraction = free sum ÷ total sum; medium supply = mg/g × g/L; fold range = max ÷ min across products reporting that amino acid. All values regenerated in data.csv / calc-notes.md.
  • Limitations: typical analyses are manufacturers' representative values, not lot certificates and not specifications; panels differ between sheets (see §2); two products publish no free fraction; no product was purchased or tested for this article; lot-to-lot variation within a product is not published by any of the five manufacturers and is therefore not estimated here. That last gap is the one a buyer's own lot profiling exists to close.
