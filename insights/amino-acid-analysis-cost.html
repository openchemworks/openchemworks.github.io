What drives the price

Free versus total amino acids

Total amino acids need acid hydrolysis first. The harmonized pharmacopeial chapter describes 6 N hydrochloric acid with 0.1–1.0 % phenol, typically at 110 °C for 24 hours under vacuum or inert atmosphere 1. Hydrolysis destroys tryptophan and partly destroys serine and threonine 1. It also deamidates asparagine and glutamine to aspartic and glutamic acid 1. Cysteine, cystine and methionine need a separate performic acid oxidation before hydrolysis 1.

So hydrolysate panels report Asx and Glx rather than four separate amino acids, and sulfur amino acids and tryptophan are often priced as separate tests. We offer both: free amino acids (AA-1, $150) and total amino acids after hydrolysis (AA-T, $230 including hydrolysis). Free versus total amino acids explains the difference.

Number of analytes

Panels differ in which amino acids they include, so compare quotes analyte by analyte, not by panel name. Our AA-1 lists all 19 amino acids and ammonia by name on the analyte reference page.

Sample preparation

Some quotes price the measurement alone and add preparation as a separate line; others include it. Ask which applies. We include clarification and deproteinization of liquid samples in AA-1. Solids and powders carry a $30 surcharge.

Calibration and matrix

A QC standard and reagent blanks run alongside every batch of samples, and they take instrument time too. Matrix matters too. Pre-column derivatization methods are more affected by buffer components than post-column methods 1. That is why a new matrix may call for a spike-recovery check. We charge $200 the first time we see a matrix type, waived at 20 or more samples.

Turnaround

Our target turnaround: 5 business days from receipt, one analysis batch per week. Measured turnaround published once validation is complete.

Minimum orders

For one or two samples, a minimum order can outweigh the per-sample rate. Ours is $150.

Report contents

Calculations, chromatograms and raw data are sometimes included and sometimes itemized. Ask what the report contains before comparing prices. Ours is a PDF with concentrations, chromatogram and QC summary. Raw data is available as CSV on request.

Estimating your total

Add four things:

  • the per-sample rate times the number of samples,
  • any one-time fees,
  • preparation surcharges,
  • any package or plan price that replaces per-sample rates.

Then check the total against the minimum order.

Here is a worked example with our posted prices: 12 cell culture supernatants, a matrix type we have not seen before, standard panel.

  • 12 samples, new matrix: 12 × $150 = $1,800, plus $200 for new-matrix qualification, for $2,000.
  • 30 samples a month: the 25+ tier applies, 30 × $125 = $3,750, and the qualification fee is waived.
  • 2 samples: 2 × $150 = $300, above the $150 minimum.
  • Total amino acids, 5 protein powders: 5 × $230 (analysis plus hydrolysis) = $1,150, plus $30 per sample for solids preparation = $1,300.
  • Creatine, 4 gummy lots: 4 × $120 = $480.

What you should get for the price

Use this checklist on any quote or report, including ours.

  • Method named: derivatization chemistry, separation and detection.
  • Scope stated: every analyte reported, and any not reported, such as tryptophan after acid hydrolysis 1.
  • Free or total stated: the two answer different questions and are not interchangeable.
  • Units and basis: µM, mg/L or per gram, with dilution factors.
  • Internal standard: the harmonized pharmacopeial chapter recommends one to monitor losses and variation 1.
  • QC standard: an amino acid mixture of known concentration run with your samples, and its result.
  • Blanks: reagent blanks alongside the samples.
  • Low values: results below the quantitation limit flagged, not reported as numbers.
  • Chromatograms: so you can see what was integrated.
  • Raw data: available on request.
  • Quality-system status: research use, GLP, GMP or ISO 17025.

Our report gives concentrations in µM and mg/L with a chromatogram and QC summary, and raw data as CSV on request. We are not a GLP, GMP or ISO 17025 laboratory; our work supports research, development and process monitoring.
