At a glance: what the numbers say

  • A complete total-amino-acid job costs $230 to $650 in published US prices. The four complete, like-for-like offerings we could verify on November 5, 2026 (OpenChemWorks AA-T $230, UC Davis food/tissue panel $368, Bio-Synthesis hydrolysis + analysis $450, Medallion Labs full screen $650) have a median of $409.
  • The same $450 Bio-Synthesis job costs $25.00 per amino acid reported. Bio-Synthesis reports 18 of the 20 standard amino acids (tryptophan and cysteine excluded after acid hydrolysis, per its own sample-preparation page). $450 / 18 = $25.00.
  • OpenChemWorks AA-T is $14.38 per amino acid reported, 42% below Bio-Synthesis per analyte. $230 / 16 reported amino acids = $14.38. The sticker gap is $220 per sample ($450 − $230), a 48.9% saving on the complete total-protein job.
  • For a free (plasma-style) panel, OpenChemWorks AA-1 at $150 is $38 below the UC Davis nonprofit rate of $188, a 20.2% saving, and at $7.50 per reported value (19 amino acids + ammonia = 20 values) it is the lowest per-analyte price in this census.
  • Hydrolysis alone is a $200 line item at Bio-Synthesis. That is 87% of the entire OpenChemWorks AA-T price ($200 / $230), before any analysis is run at Bio-Synthesis.
  • So what: Do not compare sticker prices. Compare price per amino acid reported for the exact job (free vs total, hydrolysis in or out). On that basis the spread in this census is $7.50 to $101.00 per value, a 13-fold range for what buyers often treat as one commodity test.
  • Why it matters: If you send 10 total-protein samples a year, choosing the $230 complete job over the $450 complete job saves $2,200 a year with no change in the question being answered. Price is published, verifiable, and checkable before you ship anything. Sampling and lab choice are the two costs you fully control.

Introduction

Amino acid analysis is usually bought from a price list, and the price lists do not agree on what a price covers. One laboratory's per-sample figure includes acid hydrolysis, another charges for it as a separate line, a third reports half as many amino acids. Comparing sticker prices across those lists answers the wrong question. This article answers the right one: for a defined job, done completely, what does the published market actually charge?

We built the answer as a census. Every price here was published on the laboratory's own website and re-read on November 5, 2026, and laboratories that only quote on request are listed as a gap rather than estimated. Each offering is then normalized the same way, dividing the published price by the number of amino acids the report carries, so a free panel, a total panel, and a single-analyte test can be compared on one basis.

The census table comes first, with the inclusion rule stated before it. The normalized comparison follows, then perspectives for buyers who send samples regularly, in bulk, or once. The summary box at the top carries the headline numbers; the Methods & Sources section shows where every figure came from, and the changelog records what changes when the census is re-run.

Perspectives

Buyer: cheapest complete answer, not cheapest sticker

A $101 single-analyte test is the cheapest line in the census and the most expensive per value ($101.00). A $200 hydrolysis-only service answers no analytical question at all. Define the job first (free or total? which amino acids must appear on the report?), then compare only the offerings that complete that job.

Methods: what the price differences buy

Three things separate these prices: hydrolysis (a $200 standalone line at Bio-Synthesis; an $80 component inside OpenChemWorks AA-T), tryptophan/cysteine recovery (only Medallion's three-method screen in this census includes dedicated preparations for both), and panel breadth in free analysis (physiological panels report additional related compounds beyond the 20 proteinogenic amino acids; UC Davis does not state a count on its rate page, so we do not assign one).

Market: the distribution, November 2026

Complete total jobs span $230–$650 (ratio 2.8×). Free panels span $150–$188 at the volumes most buyers send. This index will re-read every page every two weeks and log movements with dates, including cycles where nothing moved.

Honesty: what published prices leave out

Published prices exclude shipping in both directions, any grinding/drying fee (UC Davis lists $15/sample), rush fees where offered, and minimum-order effects (OpenChemWorks publishes a $150 order minimum for its creatine test; AA-1 itself meets it). None of the laboratories in this census publishes a rush price on the pages we read. That absence is reported here as a finding, not filled in.
