The short answer

  • A complete total amino acid job costs $230 to $650 in published US prices. The four like-for-like offerings we could verify (OpenChemWorks AA-T $230, UC Davis food and tissue panel $368, Bio-Synthesis hydrolysis plus analysis $450, Medallion Labs full screen $650) have a median of $409.
  • A free amino acid panel costs $150 to $188. OpenChemWorks AA-1 is $150 for 19 amino acids plus ammonia. The UC Davis plasma panel is $188 for 1 to 50 samples.
  • Compare price per reported line, not the sticker. On a like-for-like count, AA-T is $14.38 per line and the same $450 Bio-Synthesis job is $28.13 per line, so AA-T is 48.9% lower. The sticker gap is $220 per sample.
  • Add the extras before you compare. Powders that need extraction add PREP-1 at +$30 per sample. Bioreactor time courses are cheaper as a package: RUN-1 is $1,990 for one run with up to 24 time points.

Why quotes for the same test vary so much

Five things explain most of the spread. First, panels differ: one laboratory's amino acid profile may count a different set of amino acids, include or exclude ammonia, or leave out the compound you actually need. Second, free and total analysis are confused constantly: a cheap test that hydrolyses everything cannot say how much glutamate was free in the food as eaten, and a free panel cannot describe a protein's full makeup. Third, sample form matters: liquids, powders and oily blends each need different preparation, and a quote that never asks what your sample is should worry you more than one that prices preparation openly. Fourth, reporting bases differ: milligrams per gram, per 100 grams or per serving can make two correct results look far apart. Fifth, overhead differs: a laboratory that maintains a large quality system carries costs a small specialist laboratory does not.

Before you accept any quote, ask for the panel list in writing, the price for your sample form, whether preparation and any matrix check are included or extra, the reporting basis, the turnaround, and the all-in total for your exact sample count. Then compare the answers on price per reported line.

AA-1 is the lowest price per line among the amino acid analyzers and HPLC services we read, and within 42 cents of Walk-In's 36-analyte LC/MS list.

Two denominator warnings. Bio-Synthesis's headline says "up to 42 amino acids and related compounds supported" across its platform; we count what its sample-preparation page says the standard hydrolysis service quantifies. UC Davis does not publish a count on its rate page, so its per-line figures are marked as assumptions. Confirm in writing which count a quote uses.

What the price differences buy

Three things separate these prices. Hydrolysis is a $200 standalone line at Bio-Synthesis and is included in the OpenChemWorks AA-T price. Tryptophan and cysteine recovery needs dedicated preparations, and only Medallion's three-preparation screen in this census includes both. Panel breadth in free analysis varies too: physiological panels return related compounds beyond the 20 standard amino acids. A $101 single-analyte test is the cheapest line in the census and the most expensive per value.

Published prices leave things out: shipping in both directions, grinding or drying fees (UC Davis lists $15 per sample), rush fees, and minimum-order effects. None of the laboratories here publishes a rush price on the pages we read. OpenChemWorks publishes its extras (PREP-1 +$30, PREP-2 $200 per assay for a new matrix, waived at 20+ samples) and a $180 minimum order.
