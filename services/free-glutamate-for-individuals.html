Free glutamate for individuals and small makers

Free glutamate quantification for a single sample, without needing a company or a batch run. Results on stated bases, the chromatogram behind each number, and an optional matrix check on your actual sample.

Order samplesView Pricing

What is measured

Free glutamate is measured as part of the AA-1 free amino acid panel, by AQC (AccQ-Tag) pre-column derivatization with HPLC and dual UV and fluorescence detection. In plain terms, the sample is prepared without hydrolysis, its amino acids are chemically tagged so they can be detected, they are separated on a chromatography column, and each is measured against a calibrated standard run in the same system.

The panel covers 19 amino acids plus ammonia, so glutamate arrives in context, you see the wider free profile, which helps you judge whether the figure fits your ingredient list. Cysteine is excluded because it requires a separate workup, AA-3; say so before ordering if cysteine is central to your question. Taurine is not in the standard panel; order it as AA-1 (Taurine), the full AA-1 analysis with taurine added, $150. The extended panel (AA-2) is priced after a free call.

The service measures free glutamate from all sources combined. It cannot isolate how much MSG powder was added during making, glutamate from other ingredients is chemically the same once mixed. Only a recipe record answers that. If your question is about total glutamate (free plus protein-bound, released by hydrolysis), that is a different service, total amino acid analysis (AA-T); ask if unsure.

Your report shows the per-analyte results, the reporting basis beside them (such as per gram or per 100 grams, printed not assumed), and the chromatogram, the instrument trace, behind each number, so a future batch can be compared on identical terms. Glutamate, reported as glutamic acid, can also be expressed as an anhydrous MSG equivalent, with the form named deliberately: MSG exists in anhydrous and monohydrate forms with different molar masses, so an unnamed equivalent is ambiguous. The underlying glutamic acid result is always shown, so any reader can reconvert.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really send one sample as a private individual?
Yes. Single samples from individuals and small makers are the core of this service.

Will my report show an MSG number?
It shows glutamate as glutamic acid and can express it as an anhydrous MSG equivalent, with the basis stated. It does not claim to isolate added MSG from glutamate contributed by other ingredients.

Do I need matrix-effect qualification?
For a salty, dark, oily, or complex blend it is often the difference between a number and a trustworthy number. Ask for a view on your specific sample in your first email. The qualification (PREP-2) is a spike-recovery check at $200 per assay, one-time per formulation, waived at 20+ samples.

What if I need total glutamate instead?
Order AA-T, the hydrolysed total test at $230 per sample, or ask, the reply will direct you to the test that matches your question.

Will a regulator accept the report?
Reports support research and product development. OpenChemWorks is not yet accredited (ISO 17025, GLP or GMP), so check whether your regulator accepts data from a non-accredited laboratory. Every report carries per-analyte results, the chromatogram and the calibration, with results in 3 to 5 business days. Overnight turnaround available, with expedited service on request.
