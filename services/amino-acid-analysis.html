Short answer

Our amino acid analysis service prices free amino acids (AA-1) at $150 per sample, and they suit media, broth, drinks and juice. Total amino acids (AA-T) are $230 and suit proteins, hydrolysates and powders. Creatine (CR-1) is $120. Samples are measured by AQC derivatization and HPLC with UV (260 nm) and fluorescence detection. Overnight turnaround available, with expedited service on request.

Free or total amino acids: pick the test

Free amino acids are the ones dissolved in the sample today. Total amino acids include those locked inside proteins, released by acid hydrolysis first. Most media and broth questions need free. Most protein and powder questions need total.

Method and QC

Amino acids are derivatized with AQC (AccQ-Tag chemistry), separated by reversed-phase HPLC, and detected by both UV (260 nm) and fluorescence. We use both signals to confirm each peak, which gives richer information than either alone. Performance figures come from the published method; every report includes our own calibration and QC for the run.

  • Internal standard in every sample monitors derivatization and injection.
  • QC standard in every run confirms the calibration holds.
  • Sensitivity check each run, and spiked amino acids where the matrix calls for it.
  • Reagent blanks run with the samples.

Read more: how AQC derivatization works and quality control. We are not yet accredited (ISO 17025, GLP, GMP). Results support research, product development and process monitoring.

Limits of total amino acids

Acid hydrolysis (6 N HCl, 110 C, 24 h) destroys tryptophan. Cysteine and cystine are not reported in AA-T. Asparagine and glutamine convert to aspartate and glutamate and are reported with them. Methionine can partly oxidize, serine and threonine are partly lost, and valine and isoleucine bonds hydrolyze slowly, so 24 h hydrolysis can under-recover them. See acid hydrolysis.

Frequently asked questions

How much does amino acid analysis cost per sample?

Free amino acids (AA-1) are $150 per sample, or $125 at 15 or more samples a month and $99 at 30 or more a month on a 6-month plan. Total amino acids (AA-T) are $230 per sample including hydrolysis. Creatine (CR-1) is $120. Prices are published and the minimum order is $180.

Is there a minimum order or an academic rate?

The minimum order is $180. Universities and non-profits can ask about an education rate when booking.

Do you charge extra for hydrolysis or solid samples?

No. Acid hydrolysis is included in the $230 AA-T price. Powders that simply dissolve in water or buffer have no preparation charge. A solid that needs weighing, extraction and filtration adds PREP-1, $30 per sample. A new matrix can need a one-time spike-recovery check (PREP-2, $200 per assay), waived at 20 or more samples.

How long does it take?

Standard results come in 3 to 5 business days from receipt. Overnight turnaround is available, with expedited service on request. Tell us your sample count and type, and we'll confirm timing.

How much sample do I need?

Liquids such as media, broth, wort, juice and drinks need 200 µL per sample, and 400 µL is preferred. Plasma and urine need from 15 µL. Solids need at least 100 mg. Label each tube with the sample ID exactly as on the sample sheet.

Do I need free or total amino acids?

Free amino acids (AA-1) measure what is dissolved in the sample now, such as media, broth, drinks and juice. Total amino acids (AA-T) break proteins down with acid first, so they tell you the amino acid composition of proteins, hydrolysates and powders.

Why are tryptophan and cysteine missing after acid hydrolysis?

Acid hydrolysis destroys tryptophan, and cysteine and cystine are not reported in AA-T. Asparagine and glutamine convert to aspartate and glutamate and are reported with them. Cysteine and cystine need a separate reduction and alkylation workup (AA-3), priced after a free 30-minute call.

Are you accredited, and can I see a report first?

Not yet. We are not accredited to ISO 17025, GLP or GMP. Our testing supports research, product development and process monitoring, and every report includes the chromatogram, calibration and QC results. An example report is linked on this page.
