Short answer

We measure both creatine and creatinine in one porous graphitic carbon (PGC) HPLC-UV run and report % w/w or mg per serving against your label claim, with the raw chromatogram and QC. Gummies/chews (CR-1G) are $180 per product including the first-formula spike-recovery check (+$120 individual-gummy uniformity; +$100 taurine from the same prep); powders, capsules and drinks (CR-1) are $120 per sample ($180 online minimum). Results in 3–5 business days.

Both creatine and creatinine elute cleanly in one 5-minute PGC run (OCW_PGC_CRE03NF, 210/230 nm spectral ratio check) so gummy degradation is quantified directly.

Why test creatine

Creatine monohydrate is 88% creatine by weight, and typical maintenance intakes are 3–5 g a day 12. A product that delivers less than its label shortchanges that dose, and the shortfall is not visible in the tub.

Under-dosing has been documented repeatedly. In a published HPLC method study, some over-the-counter products contained very little creatine compared with their label claim, and liquid formulations showed substantial conversion of creatine to creatinine 5. In 2024 NOW Foods reported that six of 12 creatine gummies tested in-house failed label claim and flagged how hard capable gummy testing labs were to find 6; in June 2025 SuppCo independent testing likewise found 4 of 6 Amazon gummies failed (with high creatinine) while 5 of 5 powders passed.

Powders are stable; drinks and gummies need checking

As a dry powder, creatine monohydrate is very stable: creatinine stayed below the detection limit of 67 ppm after more than three years at 40 °C 3. In solution the picture changes. Creatine cyclizes to creatinine at a rate set by pH, not concentration: after 3 days at 25 °C, losses were 4% at pH 5.5, 12% at pH 4.5 and 21% at pH 3.5, while creatine was relatively stable at pH 6.5–7.5 3.

That is why ready-to-drink products and gummies are worth testing at the end of shelf life, not only at release. Manufacturing can also leave impurities from synthesis, such as dicyandiamide, dihydrotriazine and creatinine, in some raw material 4.

How the method works

  1. Preparation. Powders and capsule contents are weighed and dissolved; gummies are dissolved whole; drinks are diluted. Everything is filtered before injection.
  2. Separation and detection. Creatine is separated on a porous graphitic carbon column (Thermo Hypercarb, 50 × 2.1 mm), a phase chosen because it retains polar compounds that a C18 column lets slip through, and detected by UV absorbance at low wavelength, following published methods for creatine and creatinine in supplements 5.
  3. Quantitation. Results are calculated against a calibration made from a creatine reference standard, with a check standard in every run.

Creatine needs no derivatization, so the test is fast and the price is flat.

Optional: screen for amino acid fillers

If your concern is a creatine product cut with cheaper material, add the free amino acid profile (AA-1, $150) on the same sample. A pure creatine monohydrate should show almost no free amino acids; added glycine or other amino acids show up as peaks in the profile. See the amino acid spiking test for how that screen reads.
